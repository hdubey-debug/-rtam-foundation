# The Ṛtambhareśvara Murti — Iconography Source

*The design's own scripture: what the sanctum object is, how it reads, and what
every constructed mark in this identity must derive from.*

**Provenance.** Codified 2026-07 from the founder's brief and the sanctum
design reference (`reference/shivalinga-sanctum-reference.jpeg` — kept as
design intent, not distribution; the source of truth for marks is the vector
geometry in `geometry/`). The Latin spelling lock (`RTAM`, `Rtambhareshvara`)
is unchanged by anything in this document.

---

## 1 · The name

**Ṛtambhareśvara** = **ṛtam** (ऋतम्, the cosmic order — the oldest Vedic word
for truth as *the way things hold*) + **bhara** (भर, from √bhṛ — to bear, to
carry, to uphold) + **īśvara** (ईश्वर, the Lord).

> **The God who bears the cosmic order.**

The Foundation's name is the first word of the temple's. The temple is a Shiva
mandir; the murti below is its heart. Everything the brand does visually is a
projection of this one object.

## 2 · The object

A water-basin podium in carved stone. At its centre a black **Shivalinga**
rises from a **two-tier lotus of 24 petals** (12 upper, 12 lower, offset half a
step) that forms its **jaladhari** (the water-catching seat). Around the lotus,
an annulus of still **water**. The raised **rim** that contains the water
carries **12 sun medallions** — the Ādityas, the twelve months of the solar
wheel. One channel breaks the circle: the **nālī**, the spout that carries the
offered water out of the basin and into a small square **pool** set in the
pavement, toward the devotee. Directly above, an **oculus** admits a shaft of
light; from the gallery the priest pours the **abhisheka** stream, which falls
along that axis onto the crown of the linga.

## 3 · The three readings

The same object is engineered to read three ways. This is the brand's central
idea: **one form, three truths, depending on where you stand.**

### 3.1 Front elevation — Sāṅkhya (the cosmology)

Standing before it: the linga (**Puruṣa**, the soul, the witness) rises out of
the 24-petalled lotus (**the 24 tattvas of Prakṛti** — the categories of
manifest nature). Spirit stands *in* nature, not apart from it; nature flowers
*around* spirit, not against it. 24 + 1.

### 3.2 Plan view (from above) — the ṛta-chakra (the cosmic order)

Looking straight down the light shaft: a **wheel**. The linga becomes a dark
disc at the hub — a **bindu**. The 12 upper petals become 12 spokes. The 12
rim medallions become the wheel's outer stations — the 12 Ādityas, the year.
The wheel is ṛta itself: the order that turns. And the Lord is not outside
the wheel steering it; **He is the hub it turns on** — Ṛtambhareśvara, bearing
the order from its centre. 12 × 2 around 1.

### 3.3 The inner ring — anāhata (the heart)

Tighten the same plan view to the lotus alone: **a 12-petalled lotus around a
still centre** — the classical diagram of **anāhata**, the heart chakra. The
diagram of the sanctum *is* the diagram of the chest. What turns the cosmic
wheel is seated in the heart; the same god, the same hub.

> *The one seated in the heart's twelve-petalled lotus is the one bearing the
> twelve-stationed wheel of the year.* — the founder's brief, condensed.

## 4 · The numbers

| Number | In the murti | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | the linga / the hub / the bindu | Puruṣa; Īśvara; the still centre |
| **12** | upper lotus tier; rim medallions; anāhata petals | the Ādityas, the months, the heart's petals — one dozen, three registers |
| **24** | full lotus (two offset tiers of 12) | the tattvas of Prakṛti |
| **30°** | the angle between adjacent spokes/medallions | the wheel's step (360 ÷ 12) |
| **15°** | the offset between the two petal tiers | the half-step that makes 24 out of 12 × 2 |

These numbers are the brand's constants. The layout grid (`geometry/grid.json`)
runs on 12 columns; spacing steps on 6 / 12 / 24; constructed angles on 30° and
15°. Nothing about this is decorative numerology — it is the murti, measured.

## 5 · The vertical axis

Reading the sanctum top to bottom:

```
oculus (light)            the unmanifest, entering
  ↓ light shaft
abhisheka stream          grace, descending
  ↓
linga crown               the Lord, receiving
  ↓ (over the body of the linga)
lotus (24)                nature, flowering around spirit
  ↓
water annulus             the held offering — order as stillness
  ↓ nālī
floor pool                the offering returned to the world, toward the devotee
```

Order **descends** as light and **returns** as water. The axis is the temple's
liturgy drawn as a line. The nālī is where the circle opens to serve — the one
deliberate break in an otherwise perfect wheel, and it points **at the
devotee**.

## 6 · The bindu unification

The existing brand signature — the gold dot beneath the R — was defined
typographically (the diacritic of Ṛ). The murti gives it its real referent,
and all three are the same mark:

1. **The diacritic**: the dot under R that turns RTAM into ṚTAM.
2. **The linga from above**: the dark disc at the hub of the ṛta-chakra —
   the plan-view reading of §3.2.
3. **The falling drop**: one drop of the abhisheka stream, caught between the
   vessel and the crown — the vertical axis of §5 reduced to a point.

**The dot beneath the R is the linga seen from above and the drop seen from
the side.** This is why the bindu is drawn (a real `<circle>`, exact and
constructed) and never typed: it is not punctuation; it is the god-point of
the whole system. Every constructed mark in Phase 2 inherits its centre from
this point.

## 7 · The sanctity ladder

Not every surface may carry every reading. The system extends the existing
sacred/public register rule into a ladder — **the more literal the depiction
of the sanctum, the more consecrated the permitted context**:

| Rung | Form | Permitted contexts |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · most sacred | front elevation (linga explicit) | consecrated & ceremonial surfaces: the temple itself, prāṇa-pratiṣṭhā materials, ritual publications |
| 2 · sacred | plan-view yantra (linga abstracted to hub-bindu) | seals, certificates, ceremonial print, the Foundation's crest |
| 3 · operational | wheel reductions (ring + spokes + bindu), the Ṛ/ऋ monograms | avatars, favicons, app icons, everyday collateral |
| 4 · public | typographic wordmarks; ASCII `RTAM` | URLs, legal documents, embroidery, any context where the sacred mark cannot be governed |

Rule of thumb: **the linga's silhouette never appears where it cannot be
treated with care.** Abstraction is not dilution — it is protection.

- Palette referents (see the type-colour study): sandstone = the carved

  the water at night. The palette stops being taste and becomes a portrait of
  the sanctum.
