4 · the jala experiment — does water need its own colour?

ऋतम्भरेश्वर मंदिर
SACRED NIGHT · DEEP INDIGO #1C1A3D
ऋतस्य पन्थाम्
indigo — night sky AND the water the basin holds
ऋतम्भरेश्वर मंदिर
JALA TEAL CANDIDATE #1B3C42
ऋतस्य पन्थाम्
the teal candidate — technically passable, tonally a spa

Verdict — indigo is ratified as the water. The teal clears contrast but drags the identity toward the wellness/aquatic register this project has rejected three times; and theologically the murti's water is night-dark, holding the lamp's reflection — which is exactly ivory-on-indigo with gold. One ground colour for night and water keeps the liturgy of the palette intact. No new token.

महाशिवरात्रि महोत्सव
FESTIVAL EDITION ONLY · KUMKUM ON LIGHT, EMBER ON NIGHT

Rules: ceremonial accents appear only on festival/ritual surfaces — invitations, festival posters, prasad packaging. Never in UI, never as body text, never both at once with gold rules on the same surface except at festival scale. Kumkum is the light-ground voice (5.27 on ivory), ember the night voice (6.22 on indigo).

WHAT GOES ON THE POSTER
  1. The mark — chakra, top centre; gold at the hub is the first thing the eye finds.
  2. The name, Devanagari first — at least 2× the English support line.
  3. Gold rule — the one gold line; separates name from message.
  4. The motto — ऋतस्य पन्थाम् (becomes a governed master in Phase 3).
  5. Occasion — festival name in ceremonial colour (kumkum by day, ember by night) + tithi/date/time.
  6. Ground zone — venue, site, QR. Nothing else; emptiness is the register.
Bilingual rule: Devanagari leads on city surfaces; English leads on web. Same grid either way.
