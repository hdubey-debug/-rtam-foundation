# exp/waterline — hypothesis (Direction D, adventurous probe)

**Claim.** The sanctum's front view survives distillation to three strokes
and a point: the **drop** (gold), the **dome** (one arc), the **lotus cup**
(one arc), the **waterline** (one gold line). No enclosure, no symmetry
apparatus, maximum whitespace — the holiest possible mark that could still
hang in a modern gallery. Innovation register: highest of the four.

**What this direction bets:**

1. Reduction is reverence: fewer marks, more silence — the murti's axis
   speaks even at four elements.
2. The mark *teaches itself* (see the learnability strip: water → cup →
   dome → drop); a first-time viewer can be walked to the full sanctum in
   four steps.
3. The extreme reduction (drop over waterline) is not just a logo state —
   it is the brand's **section divider**, giving the web/print system an
   ownable ornament for free.

**Variants shipped:** mark (light / dark / mono), favicon (light / dark,
three elements), divider glyph, learnability strip.

**Risks (named honestly):** without context the mark can misread as
sunrise-over-water — wellness/spa adjacency (mitigations: the gold drop's
grammar ties it to the bindu system; usage always near the wordmark at first
exposure); it carries the least "12-ness" of the four directions (no Āditya
count, no petal count — the numbers live only in the other readings); the
cup-through-dome weave must keep exact stroke weights or it goes flabby at
small sizes.
